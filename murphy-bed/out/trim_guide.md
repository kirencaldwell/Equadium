# Trim guide

All trim values come from the parameters in `design.py`; the ones marked (assumed) need your measurements.
The Create-A-Bed plans only show trim as *options* (p.3) and give two instructions: notch the cabinet
verticals for existing baseboard (assembly booklet p.8, step 14) and apply base moulding across the front
after the bed is closed (p.11, step 20). Everything else below is our design, not the maker's.

## 1. Crown moulding (front run, 3-1/2" tall x 3-1/2" projection, assumed)

- **Match the room.** Use the same profile as the existing crown. Take a 6" offcut or a photo with a profile
  gauge from a hidden spot (closet, behind a door) to your supplier. The assumed size is a 45-degree spring
  angle crown, which has equal height and projection.
- **Stock.** One 12 ft piece. The front run is 132" wall to wall, so there is no splice.
- **Backer blocks.** Cut 3/4" ply triangles with legs of 3-1/2" x 3-1/2" (45-degree hypotenuse).
  Screw them to the tops of the towers and the bed header front rail about every 16" (about 10).
  Nail the crown to them. Do not hang the crown from the bed header alone, and do not add weight to the bed itself.
- **Cutting the ends.** No mitres are needed for a straight wall-to-wall run. Cut one end 1/16" long, hold it
  against the first side wall, mark the wall's angle, and trim to fit. Then do the other end the same way, a hair
  long first. Check with a test-fit before nailing. Walls are rarely square, so scribe each end.
- **If you leave an end exposed** (a return back to the wall): cut the crown upside down and backwards on the
  mitre saw, resting against the fence at its spring angle. For a 90-degree outside corner use mitre 35.3 degrees /
  bevel 30 degrees on a 45-degree spring crown, or mitre 31.6 / bevel 33.9 on a 38-degree one. Cut a scrap first.
- **Clearance.** New crown top is 90-5/8"; existing crown starts at 92-1/2".
  With the assumed 1" floor drop the gap is 7/8".
  Do the dry fit before cutting the crown.

## 2. Base moulding (front run)

- **Stock.** At least 5-1/4" tall: 4" showing at the high point plus 1" for the
  assumed floor drop and 1/4" extra. A 1x6 ripped to size works.
- **Scribing to the floor.** Set the cabinet level first. Mark the base's top line level at 4" above the shim
  height. Set a compass (or a block and pencil) to the largest floor gap, trace the floor onto the board, cut to the line with a
  jigsaw or block plane, and back-bevel the cut about 5 degrees so the bottom edge meets the floor tight.
- **Cutting the ends.** Square ends that butt into the existing baseboards on the side walls, scribed like the crown.
- **Fastening.** Glue and finish-nail it to the front edges of the tower bottoms. Do not nail it to the bed's face panel.
- **Bed clearance.** The face panel bottom sits about 2-5/8" above the floor, so the base must stand in front of it
  (the kit's p.21 drawing). Confirm with Create-A-Bed before you rely on that.

## 3. Baseboard notches (kit step 14, extended to the towers)

If you keep the existing baseboard (assumed 4-1/2" tall x 1/2" thick), notch the rear bottom of each bed
vertical **and** each tower side: **4-5/8" tall x 5/8" deep**. For the 1/4" tower backs, cut the bottom edge
4-5/8" short instead. Simplest alternative: remove the baseboard behind the unit and patch it later.

## 4. Edge banding (the "F" edges)

- Use 13/16" iron-on veneer: bed face panels (long edges), cabinet verticals (front edges), header rails, tower fronts, shelf fronts.
- Iron slowly at medium-high heat, press with a block, trim the overhang with a banding trimmer or sharp chisel,
  and lightly sand the edge flush. Do the end grain first, then the long edges.

## 5. Face-panel moulding (mitred frames in the crown's profile family)

- **Profile.** Ask the supplier for panel (picture-frame) moulding in the same family as your crown: same maker or the same curve, about 3/4 x 2 stock. Take a 6 inch offcut of the existing crown with you so they can match the curve.
- **Layout.** Four mitred frames, one per panel, 8 stiles and 8 rails. Each frame is 15-9/16" wide outside, with 1/4" between frames. The centre gap sits over the seam between the two 32 inch face panels, so no frame crosses it.
  Keep everything 1/2" inside the side and top edges so nothing catches on the cabinet verticals or the header as the bed swings, and start the
  bottom rail 1-1/2" up from the panel's bottom edge so it clears the 4" base moulding.
- **Cutting.** Mitre both ends at 45 degrees, measuring each piece to the long point (stiles 79-3/4", rails 15-9/16"). Cut all eight stiles and eight rails from one setup so the frames match, cut a spare of each, and dry-fit a frame on the panel before gluing. Check the mitres on a flat surface first.
- **Fastening.** Glue and 1-1/4 inch 18 ga brads into the plywood panel only (no longer than 1-1/4 inch: 3/4 inch of moulding plus 1/2 inch into the 3/4 inch panel, so nothing pokes through the back). The moulding stands 3/4" proud of the panel, which puts it 5/8" proud of the cabinet front, a raised-panel look. It still passes under the header rail with 1/2" to spare.
- **Handles** (5 to 6 feet high, kit p.21): bars 4" either side of the centre seam, inside the two middle panels, which leaves 1-5/8" to the nearest moulding.
- **Weight.** About 12.4 lb of moulding in solid poplar (the cross-section is an estimate, so weigh a sample piece). The kit sets the queen springs at #5 on a 1-5 scale and its instructions only describe turning the setting *down*
  (assembly booklet p.11), so the springs have no extra power in reserve. Add the moulding **before** you balance the bed, and choose a mattress that, with the moulding,
  stays at or below 80 lb: 67 lb or lighter. If the mattress you want is heavier, thin the moulding or go back to the flat-strip option.
  Create-A-Bed's advice is to call them before modifying the bed, so this is your call to make; a quick call about the springs is cheap insurance.

## 6. Order of work

1. Remove or notch the baseboard. 2. Set the bed cabinet level and plumb on shims and anchor it into studs.
3. Set the towers to its height and tie them to the cabinet and wall. 4. Fit and scribe the scribe strips to the side walls.
5. Fit the crown (dry fit first), then the base moulding. 6. Caulk, fill, and paint.
